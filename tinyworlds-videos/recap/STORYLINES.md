# Storylines: the recap and the invitation

What the film must leave a stranger with: thirteen real cities are free to explore and to
learn from, six of them exist because people paid for them, and the next one can carry
their name.

Facts it may say (as of 2026-11-12, from the live API and the data folders):

- 13 real cities on the map, in 4 countries (the fan-made places stay out: the project's rule
  for what promotes it).
- 6 of them brought by their benefactors (Dallas, Cincinnati, Pittsburgh, Philadelphia,
  Colorado Springs, Truckee): 9 benefactors, US$225.
- A city is built when its gifts reach US$30: one stone per dollar.
- 3,248 buildings light up with their Wikipedia opening, in 7 cities; Guess the building in 6.
- About 49,800 places to search; three interface languages.
- About 28,300 visits to the city maps, as the front page's cards count them.
- Waiting for their first stone: London, Paris, Tokyo, Lisbon, Buenos Aires, São Paulo.
- The site's own words: "Whole cities, pocket-sized", "Brought to you by 2 benefactors, who
  donated $35", "The First Stone is still unclaimed", "Your name here".

All three: 16:9, 1920 x 1080, 30 fps, about 45-55 s, narrated (ElevenLabs), subtitles in the
site's pixel letters (Tiny5) on its black enamel signs, the spoken word lit in the accent,
stepping in like the site's signs (pixels do not ease). Every map is the real tiles or the real
viewer, every page the real page.

## A. Thirty stones (recommended)

**Device:** a number lands: US$30, one stone a dollar, is a whole city. **Form:** an
object-to-object chain, the stone as the carrier.

Dallas's real foundation fills in two gifts, the keystone drops and Dallas itself rises out of
it; we go into that city to see what the stones paid for (search, a building that tells its
story, the game), then out to all thirteen, the six with gold plaques, the names in the Hall of
Fame, and last London's foundation, empty, its first stone waiting. The viewer leaves knowing
what thirty dollars does, and that the next stone is theirs to lay.

- **First 2 s:** the fog, Dallas's foundation (the site's own stone wall, 30 pixel stones)
  with ten stones already lit; the next twenty land on the beat, "30 of 30 stones laid".
  Voice: "Two people built this city."
- **Beats:** hook (the stones, two gifts) -> reveal (the keystone; Dallas rises: "Whole cities,
  pocket-sized") -> proof in Dallas (search flies, Reunion Tower lights with its Wikipedia
  opening, Guess the building) -> payoff (out to the front page: thirteen cities alive, six gold
  plaques, the figures) -> claim (the Hall of Fame, their names kept with their cities) -> end
  card (London's empty foundation, "The First Stone is still unclaimed", the address).
- **Look:** the site itself: fog #e9e6df, black enamel signs, Archivo and Tiny5, taxi yellow
  rationed, gold only for what benefactors brought.
- **Sound:** a warm plucked bed that builds as stones are laid; a stone tick per stone, a low
  thud for the keystone; the music opens fully at the payoff.
- **Narration (draft, ~95 words):** "Two people built this city. Thirty dollars, one stone each.
  When the last one is laid, the city goes up: every street, drawn by code from open data.
  Search anywhere. Point at a building and it tells you its story. Then guess it, and learn the
  city as you play. That's thirteen cities so far, free for everyone. Six of them were paid for
  by the people who wanted them, and their names stay with them in the Hall of Fame. London has
  no stones yet. Lay the first one, and become a permanent benefactor."

## B. Thirteen

**Device:** a number counted up: one city a beat, thirteen of them. **Form:** cut on the beat.

A brisk tour of the real cities, each one's own flyover cutting in on the beat with its
wordmark and its colour, the count climbing in big pixel numerals; then three verbs on three
beats (explore, learn, play) in the real viewer, the six benefactor-built cities flashing gold,
and "number fourteen" left blank for the viewer. It feels like an achievement reel: fast,
proud, and it ends on a question.

- **First 2 s:** New York's hero flyover full frame, "1" in Tiny5 at the left, tinynyc's
  wordmark sign stepping in; on the next beat a hard cut to Ribeirão Preto and "2".
- **Beats:** hook (1, New York) -> the tour (to 13, Truckee) -> reveal ("Whole cities,
  pocket-sized") -> proof (explore, learn, play: one shot each in the viewer) -> payoff (six
  cards turn gold: "9 benefactors, $225") -> claim -> end card ("14", blank, "Which city
  next?", the address).
- **Look:** each city's own accent as the field it cuts on, the real flyovers, huge Tiny5
  numerals.
- **Sound:** a driving 120 BPM bed, a hit on every cut (two or three rotated); the voice rides
  over it in short lines.
- **Narration (draft):** "New York. Ribeirão Preto. Orlando. ... Truckee. Thirteen cities, drawn
  by code from open data, free to explore. Search any street. Read about any landmark. Play to
  learn them. Six were built because people paid for them: nine benefactors. Number fourteen
  is up to you."

## C. Guess the building (the bold one)

**Device:** a question with the input: the film plays the site's own game with the viewer.
**Form:** one frame that rebuilds: the quiz card stays put while the city behind it changes.

Three real rounds of Guess the building, in three cities: a Wikipedia opening with the name
blanked, four names, a beat for the viewer to guess, the answer lit and the map flies to it.
Between rounds the voice says what the viewer just learned and where they are; the last
question is the film's own: "Which city should be next?", and the answer is the viewer's gift.

- **First 2 s:** the real quiz card already up over a lit building at dusk, its text with the
  name blanked, "round 1 of 3", four names; the pointer hovers. Voice: "Can you guess this
  building?"
- **Beats:** hook (round 1, Philadelphia) -> round 2 (Pittsburgh) -> round 3 (Dallas) -> reveal
  (pull back to the front page: thirteen cities to learn from) -> payoff (the gold plaques: who
  built these) -> claim -> end card ("Which city should be next?", London, Paris, Tokyo, the
  address).
- **Look:** the viewer's black enamel card as the one home stage; the city's own accent changes
  with each round.
- **Sound:** a light ticking pulse under each question, a soft hit on the answer, the music
  opening at the pull back.
- **Narration (draft):** "Can you guess this building? ... It's Philadelphia's City Hall. Every
  building that lights up here tells its story, in thirteen cities drawn from open data. ...
  These cities are here because people paid for them. Which one should be next?"
- **Cost:** the most filming of the three (the viewer in three cities, the quiz played in each).
